## 7. FINANCES (state debt and budget)

**Heading:** State Finances — what Andhra Pradesh owes
**Intro:** AP's debt since bifurcation, told from three independent official
source families side by side — RBI, the state's own budget books, and CAG audits.
Where the sources disagree, the disagreement is the content.

**Verification chain (explained on the page):** A budget estimate (BE) is a plan →
the revised estimate (RE) is the government's own mid-year correction → the
audited actual (A) is money that really moved, certified by the CAG. Charts mark
the stage of every figure; solid means audited. "We never take spending figures
from news articles."

**Section: The three official answers to one question**
The same year's debt as RBI, the state budget, and CAG each publish it.

**Section: What the debt is made of**
"A government doesn't take one loan — it borrows in many ways."
Audited three-way split per year (CAG): Internal debt · Loans from the Centre ·
Public account.
"Public Account = money the government holds and owes back — employees' provident
funds, deposits, reserve funds."
Then the full end-March 2024 breakdown (RBI Statement 18), including:
Market loans — bonds investors buy (SDLs) ₹3,66,564.5 Cr · Loans from the central
government ₹33,332 Cr · Employees' provident fund ₹29,791.7 Cr · Deposits ₹28,951.6 Cr ·
UDAY power loans · Reserve funds · Small-savings (NSSF) securities.
Facts shown alongside:
- "Market loans (SDLs) are the biggest and fastest-growing piece in rupees:
  ₹1.89 lakh crore (March 2020) → ₹4.24 lakh crore (March 2025 revised estimate)
  — up 124%."
- "Loans from the central government grew fastest in proportion: ₹17,763 crore
  (2020) → ₹48,104 crore (2025 RE), about 2.7 times."
- "Small-savings (NSSF) securities shrank every single year — ₹11,331 crore (2020)
  → ₹5,507 crore (2025 RE)."
- "Across CAG's audited span (2016→2023): internal debt grew 2.9×, central loans
  2.1×, public-account liabilities 1.4× — and the fastest-growing audited category
  of all is off-budget borrowing: ₹26,097 crore (2020) → ₹1,28,048 crore (2023),
  4.9× in three years."

**Section: The debt outside the books**
"Borrowings taken through state corporations — CAG says these are NOT included in
the headline debt above."
"In CAG's own words, these borrowings 'do not find mention in the Finance Accounts'
and stayed 'outside legislative control'. They grew from ₹26,097 crore (March 2020)
to ₹1,28,048 crore (March 2023) — and CAG states that counting them takes the real
debt burden to 41.89% of the state's economy in 2022-23, against ~33% without them.
This is why two people quoting 'the debt' can both point at official numbers and
still differ by a lakh crore."
"Awaiting the next audit:" — where a figure exists only in a press report, it is
held out of the charts and shown as pending.

**Section: Deficits year by year** — fiscal deficit and revenue deficit, each
marked BE / RE / audited.

**Section: Guarantees** — money the government has promised to repay if someone
else defaults.

**Section: What the white papers claim**
Political white papers shown strictly as claims, with the context to check them
against the audited record — never mixed into the charts.

**Every section carries:** an "explain this simply" note and its own list of
official source documents.

## 9. ALL STATES — CENTRAL RECORDS

**Heading:** All States — Central Records | అన్ని రాష్ట్రాలు · కేంద్ర గణాంకాలు

**Intro:** "The Centre's own statistical tables list every state, every year. This
is where Andhra Pradesh actually stands — not where a speech says it stands. Pick
a measure and a year; we rank the table as published and mark AP. A dash means the
Centre has not published that figure."

**Source line:** RBI Handbook Table 19 (per-capita income) · Table 21 (GSDP) ·
State Finances Statement 20 (debt). Link across to Governments for AP's own tenure
record.

**Three measures, each with its own plain-words explanation:**

*Per-person income (per-capita NSDP)* — ₹ per person per year, higher is better.
"Take everything a state's economy produced in a year (minus wear-and-tear of
machines and buildings), divide by its population — that is per-capita NSDP. It is
the fairest single number for comparing big and small states, because Uttar Pradesh
producing more than Sikkim in total says nothing about how the average person is
doing. 'Current prices' means the rupee figures of that year itself, not adjusted
for inflation."

*State GDP (GSDP)* — ₹ crore, higher is bigger. "GSDP is the value of everything
produced inside the state in a year — the state's total economic size. Bigger states
naturally have bigger GSDP, so use this to see scale, and use per-person income to
see how the average citizen fares."
Note: "RBI prints this table in ₹ lakh; we show ₹ crore (divided by 100, nothing
else changed)."

*Government debt, as % of state GDP* — lower is safer. "A state's outstanding
liabilities measured against the size of its economy — like comparing a family's
loans to its yearly income. ₹1 lakh crore of debt is heavy for a small economy and
light for a huge one, so this ratio is how debt burdens are honestly compared across
states. The 15th Finance Commission's glide path pointed states toward roughly the
low 30s or below."
Anomaly note: "Shown exactly as RBI prints it. AP's 2012–2014 figures reflect
undivided Andhra Pradesh's liabilities, and the 2017 column carries RBI's
apportionment artifacts from the AP–Telangana split (AP 37.2% while Telangana shows
12.4%). We do not smooth or correct a published table — we tell you why it looks odd."

**Section: Every state, ranked — pick a measure and a year**
All states listed in rank order for the chosen year, AP marked.
"N states published no figure for this year and are not on the chart — a gap stays
a gap."

**Section: States side by side, every year**
Pick up to six states; each is shown across the full series. Default selection:
Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu.
"Where a line skips a year, that state published nothing for it."

**Section: The Centre's own scorecards, state by state**
Index tables the Union government's institutions publish for all states, shown
exactly as printed. "Only editions whose full all-states table is published are
charted here."

**Section: Read honestly**
"For 2011-12 to 2013-14 the income tables carry the reorganised-AP back-series
(Telangana shown separately even before the June 2014 split), as the National
Statistical Office publishes them. The debt table is the exception — its pre-split
AP rows are undivided-AP."
"Income and GSDP are at current prices (no inflation adjustment); the debt ratio's
last two columns are the states' own revised/budget estimates, marked RE/BE wherever
they appear."
